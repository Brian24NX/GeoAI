Weekly Update, 9–13 Nov 2026

Brian Zhou · CSE 5999 hiking-speed project · for Danni Beaulieu · 13 Nov 2026

This week I followed up on Fig. 1's orange dot. The Bay Area was built differently from the other regions. I tried to make it the same, found it couldn't be done at a sane cost, and dropped it, so all 16 regions now come from the same sources. Along the way I fixed two things that change every region a little, so everything is being rebuilt and the new numbers come this weekend. I also checked whether terrain needs lidar-quality elevation. It needs accurate bare-earth elevation, not fine resolution.

So the Bay Area had no anonymous uploads and nothing from before 2016 or after 2022. To make it the same, I changed the download script: a box the API can't answer is split into quarters, again if needed, down to about 1 km. That worked, and the Bay Area downloaded. But after 20 hours and 6.8 GB only 37% of its area was done, and almost all of it was car logs:

The hikes are spread through the whole region (67 of the 100 boxes not yet downloaded had hikes in October's build), so leaving out the city boxes wouldn't have saved much. Finishing would have taken another 10–20 GB and 2–3 days.

Decision: I dropped the Bay Area. It was 6% of hiking traces (177 of 2,876) and 2.5% of walking hours (117 of 4,702). The paper now has 16 regions, all from the full OSM download plus the lab crawl, and Fig. 1 has one color.

What the lab crawl itself is missing (this affects every region): there are no files for its 2008–2015 upload lists (1,031,004 uploads), none for about 15% of the 2016–19 uploads (57,291) and 25% of the 2020–22 uploads (395,646), and nothing after 2022. The API returns these public uploads' points but without timestamps, so they can't give speeds. I've asked Jonathan Lin whether the missing files can be recovered.

Two fixes that change every region a little

  1. Timeouts. The same server timeout had cut four tiles short in other regions, before their oldest uploads. Front Range and Cascades reached back only to 2009; Shenandoah and San Gabriels only to mid-2023, because three years of car logs fill their first 500+ pages. With the split boxes they are downloading again now. Where October's pages still match the server, they are reused instead of downloaded again.
  2. Anonymous recordings. The API returns an anonymous recording as one piece per box, and the builder rejoins the pieces. It missed recordings that leave a box and come back, which smaller boxes make common. Fixing that changes the counts more than the data. In the White Mountains, 147 anonymous traces become 125 and 218 hiking outings become 210. The trail segments the models use change by −0.1% to +1.6% across the six regions I compared.

So all 16 regions are rebuilt with the same code. October's builds are kept for comparison.

Does terrain need lidar?

Averaging our 5 m lidar grids to 10 m loses nothing, and at 30 m terrain keeps most of its edge over trail tags. The free global 30 m DEM (Copernicus, from radar) loses it. Along trails it sits 1–19 m above the lidar ground, because the radar sees the forest canopy, and its slope is much noisier (correlation with ours 0.85, against 0.97 for 30 m averages). So terrain needs accurate bare-earth elevation, not fine resolution. In the paper this is one Robustness sentence and one Limits clause. These numbers will be rerun with the rebuilt data.
