Weekly Update, 9–13 Nov 2026

Brian Zhou · CSE 5999 hiking-speed project · for Danni Beaulieu · 13 Nov 2026

In short: I removed the Bay Area and fixed two small bugs, so I'm rebuilding the data. The new numbers come this weekend. I also found that terrain needs the real ground height, not super-sharp maps.

1. I removed the Bay Area

It was the orange dot on the map: the OSM server kept giving up on it, so its data came only from the lab's copy. I made the download ask for smaller boxes, and that worked. But after 20 hours only a third was done, and 96% of the data came from two accounts that log car drives, which we throw away anyway. So I removed it. It was only 6% of our hikes. Now all 16 regions get their data the same way.

2. I fixed two small bugs

  • In four areas, the OSM server gave up partway, so some older hikes were missing. I downloaded them again.
  • Some anonymous hikes that crossed the edge of a download box were cut into pieces and counted more than once. Now the pieces are joined back together.

So I'm rebuilding all 16 regions. Some counts drop a little (White Mountains: 218 hikes become 210), but the trail data the models use changed by less than 2% in the six regions I compared.

3. How sharp does the elevation map need to be?

Our terrain features use very sharp lidar maps. I blurred them on purpose and also tried the free global map:

Blurred to 10 m, nothing is lost. Blurred to 30 m, terrain still beats the trail tags. The free global map (Copernicus, made by radar) does not: in forests it often sees the treetops instead of the ground. So terrain needs the real ground height, not super-sharp maps.

4. Other news

  • Jonathan's answer: the lab has older GPS files (2008–2016), from a dataset OSM published itself, and nothing after 2022. I'll ask Jonathan if they still have times. If they do, we could add more hikes to every region.
  • The paper: the IGARSS 2027 template is out, and our draft already fits it (4 pages plus references). It's due 10 Jan; I plan to submit around 8 Jan.
