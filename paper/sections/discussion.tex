\todo{Why tags do not transfer (tested, decision \#105): not because tag
values mean different things. Within the same hike, harder sac\_scale is
slower in every region where it is used ($-5\%$ mountain\_hiking vs hiking,
10 of 10 regions; $-11\%$ for harder ones, 8 of 8), and paved is faster
(10 of 10). What does not carry over is whether a tag is there: sac\_scale
is on 1--96\% of walked trail by region, and an untagged trail is faster
than a tagged one in some regions and slower in others ($-3.5$ to $+5.1\%$
within a hike). A model trained elsewhere must guess what a missing tag
means. Elevation-derived terrain exists on every trail, and its effect has
the same sign in all 17 regions ($-12\%$, steppiest vs smoothest fifth).}

\todo{What terrain captures: uneven grade within the segment (rock steps,
short pitches) that one average slope hides, plus difficulty beyond grade.
It matters most going down and on the level, where footing sets the pace,
and least on steep climbs, where effort does.
STRIDE's micro-roughness, calibrated off-trail, over-penalizes trails.}

\todo{Practical output: Eq.~(1) runs anywhere a DEM exists (Fig.~\ref{fig:trailmap});
travel-mode multipliers fail for bikes (ratio $\sim$4$\times$ on descents,
$\sim$1.6$\times$ on climbs).}

\todo{Limits: OSM uploaders are not a random sample of hikers; sparse
traces read slow, a measurement effect: thinning dense walks to 30~s between
points lowers measured speed by 11\% (straight lines cut corners; short
stops count as moving), and the curve without traces logged every $>$10~s
peaks 3\% higher (hiking functions depend on the sampling
resolution~\cite{goodwin2025application}); per-segment
effects are a few percent because GPS noise and the hiker dominate; no
weather or season covariates yet (they mattered in Hong
Kong~\cite{huang2026modeling}); lidar understory density not tested
(point-cloud cost).}
