\textbf{Target and covariates.} We predict the distribution of $\ln v$,
the log moving speed of a walking segment. Covariates come in five groups:
(a)~slope; (b)~OSM tags: sac\_scale, trail\_visibility, surface, width,
smoothness, mtb\_scale, highway type and permitted modes; (c)~terrain from
elevation, read every 1~m along the segment's matched path: the spread (SD)
and maximum of its 5~m pitches, the relief around an 11~m moving average
and the side slope, from the 5~m grid and, where available, 1~m lidar,
which adds STRIDE's ground roughness~\cite{campbell2024stride}; (d)~2021
land cover and tree canopy at 30~m~\cite{dewitz2023nlcd}; (e)~2022
AlphaEarth satellite embeddings at the segment
midpoint~\cite{brown2025alphaearth,alphaearth2025}. As a control,
``hidden climbing'' is the extra time Tobler's function predicts when the
segment is walked as ten 5~m pieces instead of at its average grade.

\textbf{Models.} The baselines are Tobler's function~\cite{tobler1993},
the published median curve of Campbell et al.~\cite{campbell2022variability},
STRIDE~\cite{campbell2024stride} as published and with its form refitted,
and our reference: a rounded, asymmetric slope curve refitted to the
training data, $\ln v = a - k_\pm\big(\sqrt{(s+s_0)^2+e^2}-e\big)$, with
$k_+$ above the peak and $k_-$ below it. Each covariate group then enters
two models: an additive one (ridge regression of the curve's residuals on
one-hot bins: deciles of continuous covariates, one level per tag value plus
``missing'') and gradient-boosted trees on slope and the covariates. The
terrain-adjusted hiking function keeps Tobler's form but lets peak speed
and slope sensitivity depend on one DEM-derived number, the steppiness
$z$:
\begin{equation}
\ln v = a_0 + a_1 z - (k_0 + k_1 z)\,|s + s_0|,
\label{eq:terrain}
\end{equation}
with $z$ the SD of the 5~m pitches along the path divided by 0.10.

\textbf{Predictive distributions and tests.} Every model predicts its
point estimate plus the quantiles of its residuals on held-out outings of
the training data (training outings split 70/15/15 into fitting, early
stopping and calibration), so calibration has to transfer too. Splits never
separate a segment from its hike: 5 folds of outings (new hikers), 5 folds
of 10~km UTM blocks (new places) and, the headline, leave one region out
(17 folds); stricter checks hold out whole zones or half the continent. We
report the continuous ranked probability score
(CRPS)~\cite{gneiting2007strictly} of $\ln v$ as skill against the refitted
slope curve, $1-\mathrm{CRPS}/\mathrm{CRPS}_{\mathrm{slope}}$, with 95\%
intervals that resample outings (whole regions for unseen regions); the
coverage of 90\% intervals; and the median error in moving time over whole
hikes after their first kilometre. A reference shows how much is the hiker:
the median residual of a hike's first kilometre, shrunk by a factor fitted
on calibration outings (0.61), applied to the rest of the hike. Asymmetry
is measured on out-and-back pairs, the same outing on the same OSM way in
both directions (mean grade at least 3\%), as the ratio of speed down to
speed up.
