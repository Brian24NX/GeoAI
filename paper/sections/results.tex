\textbf{Tobler's function fails everywhere.} Walking speed peaks near
4.2~km/h on level to gently descending trail, not at 6~km/h
(Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}a): Tobler's function is about 20\% too fast within
$\pm$10\% grade and 18\% too slow on climbs steeper than 25\%, and in
unseen regions it scores $-11.8\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:skill}), worse in all
16. Campbell et al.'s published median curve~\cite{campbell2022variability}
is about 20\% too fast on climbs steeper than 25\%; it ties our refit on
average ($+0.1\%$) but ranges by region from $-3.6\%$ (Adirondacks) to
$+3.2\%$ (Olympic). Pooled over unseen regions, every model's 90\%
intervals cover 88--89\% of segments (as little as 81\% in the North
Shore).

\textbf{Downhill is faster, but less than Tobler implies.} For the same
hiker on the same trail (2,688 out-and-back pairs from 1,084 outings;
Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}b), descending is 8\% faster than climbing at
3--10\% grade and 21--24\% above 20\%, not the 42\% Tobler implies. Our
pooled curve implies nearly the same ratios, so its asymmetry is not an
artifact of who walks which slopes.

\textbf{Map tags transfer less well.} OSM tags improve on the slope curve
for new hikes in known regions ($+1.3\%$ / $+1.4\%$, additive / trees). In
unseen regions the additive model keeps about 60\% of that ($+0.8\%$
[0.0, 1.6]) and the trees lose most of it ($+0.2\%$ [$-1.1$, 1.3]).

\textbf{Terrain transfers.} Terrain from elevation gains nearly as much in
unseen regions as within them, $+2.1\%$ [1.3, 3.2] / $+2.8\%$ [1.8, 4.2]. It
beats tags in 14 of 16 held-out regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}b; North
Shore is a tie; 13 of 16 with trees) and, on average, even a slope curve refitted on the held-out
region's own other hikers ($+1.7\%$). Terrain minus tags is $+0.6$ to
$+2.4$ points (95\% interval), above zero in every bootstrap draw. Uneven
grade inside the segment, which one average slope hides, carries about half
of the gain: the hidden-climbing control alone gives $+1.0\%$ / $+1.3\%$.
Terrain is neither a region
proxy (within regions it adds $\sim$1.8 points to a learned offset per
region) nor a hiker proxy (it adds $\sim$2.0 points to the hiker's own
pace).

No other input transfers as well. 30~m land cover and canopy add nothing within
regions and make unseen ones worse ($-0.6\%$ / $-1.4\%$).
AlphaEarth embeddings help about half as much as terrain, within regions
($+1.0\%$ / $+1.9\%$) and, less reliably, in unseen ones ($+1.3\%$
[0.2, 2.9] / $+1.2\%$ [$-0.2$, 3.0]).

Vegetation from the lidar
point clouds adds at most $+0.25$ points to terrain, within or across the
14 regions (in unseen regions the trees even lose 0.3). Terrain + tags gives $+2.6\%$ / $+3.0\%$ in unseen regions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}a).
Published STRIDE~\cite{campbell2024stride} scores $-16.0\%$; refitted,
$+0.2\%$.

\textbf{Robustness.} The terrain gain holds for newer hikes (trained to
2019, tested on 2020 on: $+2.1\%$ / $+2.9\%$; tags $+0.7\%$ / $+1.1\%$), at
every GPS recording rate ($+1.7\%$ to $+2.4\%$ in unseen regions,
additive) and with no
neighbouring region in training: with a whole zone held out, $+1.8\%$ /
$+2.5\%$ (tags $+0.8\%$ / $-0.0\%$); from West to East and back, $+2.1\%$
/ $+2.5\%$ (tags $+0.8\%$ / $-0.4\%$). It also holds for hikers the
model never saw: holding out uploaders instead of outings changes little
($+2.2\%$ / $+3.1\%$), and leaving out a region together with everyone
who hiked there (0.1--8.8\% of the training data) gives $+1.9\%$ /
$+2.5\%$ (tags $+0.4\%$ / $+0.2\%$), above tags in every draw.

\textbf{Whole hikes: the hiker dominates.} Over 1,288 hike traces in held-out
regions, the median error in moving time after the first kilometre is
13.5\% for the slope curve and 13.1\% with terrain, but 9.9\% with the
hiker's pace on that kilometre and 9.3\% with both.
