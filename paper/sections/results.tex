\todo{R1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}a, Table~\ref{tab:skill}): Tobler fails
everywhere ($-12.5\%$). Walking speed peaks near 4.2~km/h on level to
gently descending trail, not 6: Tobler is $\sim$20\% too fast within
$\pm$10\% grade and $\sim$18\% too slow on climbs steeper than 25\%.
Campbell's published median curve matches near level but is $\sim$20\% too
fast on climbs steeper than 25\%; it ties our refit on average, ranging by
region from $-3.8\%$ (White Mountains) to $+5.8\%$ (Shenandoah).}

\todo{R1b, asymmetry (Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}b): on 2,763 out-and-back pairs
(same hiker, same trail; 1,145 outings) descending is 8\% faster than
climbing at 3--10\% grade, 22--23\% above 20\%; Tobler implies 42\% at
every grade above 5\%. Our pooled curve implies nearly the same ratios as
the pairs, so its asymmetry is not a sampling artifact; Campbell's curve
implies less (2--16\%).}

\todo{R2: tags do not transfer: $+1.3\%$ within regions, $+0.9\%$ / $+0.4\%$
in unseen ones.}

\todo{R3: terrain transfers: $+2.1\%$ / $+2.8\%$ in unseen regions, beating
tags in 16 of 17 (Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}b); terrain minus tags $+0.7$ to
$+2.2$, above zero in every bootstrap draw. About 60\% of the gain is
uneven grade inside the segment. Not a region proxy (adds $\sim$1.9 points
over region offsets), not a hiker proxy. 1~m lidar $\approx$ 5~m; STRIDE's
roughness adds nothing on trails; 30~m vegetation adds nothing; embeddings
help within regions but transfer less reliably. Understory density and
canopy height from the lidar point clouds (15 regions) help within a region
(White Mountains: $+3.6\% \to +5.0\%$ in new 10~km squares) but not in
unseen regions ($+0.2$ points over terrain, helping in 9 of 15); STRIDE's
published density term over-penalises trails ($-18\% \to -35\%$).}

\todo{R3 robustness: the gain holds for newer hikes (trained up to 2019,
tested on 2020 and later: $+2.2\%$ / $+2.9\%$; tags $+0.9\%$) and at
every GPS recording rate (unseen regions, traces every $\le$2~s, 2--10~s,
$>$10~s: $+1.7$, $+2.4$, $+1.9\%$). By slope it is largest on descents and
level trail ($+2.3$ to $+2.8\%$) and smallest on steep climbs ($+0.8\%$).
It holds with no neighbouring region in training: a whole zone held out
(5 zones) $+1.9\%$ / $+2.7\%$, trained on the West and tested on the East
and back $+2.6\%$ / $+3.3\%$; tags $+0.7\%$ / $-0.1\%$ and $+1.5\%$ /
$-0.3\%$; terrain minus tags above zero in every draw.}

\todo{R4: the hiker dominates whole-hike time (1,354 hikes in held-out
regions; figure dropped for space, \texttt{fig\_hike\_time.png}):
median error 13.6\% (slope) $\to$ 13.0\% (+ terrain); the hiker's first
km brings it to 10.0\%, and 9.6\% with terrain.}

\todo{R5, travel mode (plan claim 5): one curve times a per-mode multiplier
predicts held-out runners as well as their own curve ($\times$2.5;
$+0.1\%$), but not bikes and skis: their own curve is 3.8\% better, and a
constant $\times$3.2 is 15\% too slow downhill and 35\% too fast uphill.}
